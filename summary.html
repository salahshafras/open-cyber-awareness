🎓 Key Learnings

📧 Email Phishing (Phishing)

  • Check sender addresses carefully – Scammers use look-alike domains (e.g., nexatrust-verify.com instead of nexatrust.com)
  • Hover over links before clicking – URLs in phishing emails often don't match the displayed text
  • Watch for urgency and threats – "Your account will be closed in 24 hours" is a red flag
  • Look for spelling and grammar errors – Legitimate companies proofread their communications
  • Verify unexpected requests independently – Call the company using official contact information

📱 SMS Phishing (Smishing)

  • Verify sender numbers – Legitimate companies use verified short codes, not random numbers
  • Don't click suspicious links in texts – Go directly to the official app or website instead
  • Be wary of urgent payment requests – Banks and delivery services don't demand immediate payment via SMS
  • Never reply to suspicious messages – Replying confirms your number is active
  • Check for misspelled company names – Look for subtle changes in domain names

📞 Voice Phishing (Vishing)

  • Never share passwords or MFA codes over the phone – Legitimate IT departments never ask for these
  • Don't trust caller ID alone – Phone numbers can be easily spoofed
  • Verify the caller independently – Hang up and call back using official numbers
  • Be suspicious of high-pressure tactics – Scammers create panic to bypass your judgment
  • Take your time – Real companies allow you to verify and call back later

🛡️ Best Practices to Stay Safe

🔍

Think Before You Click

Always verify the sender and inspect links before clicking. If something seems off, it probably is.

🔐

Use Multi-Factor Authentication

Enable MFA on all accounts. Even if passwords are compromised, MFA provides an extra layer of security.

📢

Report Suspicious Activity

Report phishing attempts to your IT security team immediately. You could prevent others from falling victim.

📚

Stay Updated

Phishing tactics evolve constantly. Keep learning about new threats and security best practices.

☎️

Verify Through Official Channels

When in doubt, contact the organization directly using official contact information from their website.

🧠

Trust Your Instincts

If a message creates urgency, demands sensitive info, or feels suspicious – pause and verify.

🏢 Remember: NexaTrust.com Example

Throughout this training, we used NexaTrust.com as our example company. Apply these same principles to your actual workplace:

  • Know your company's official email domains and phone numbers
  • Understand your IT department's legitimate communication channels
  • Be aware of your company's security policies and procedures
  • Know who to contact when you suspect a phishing attempt
